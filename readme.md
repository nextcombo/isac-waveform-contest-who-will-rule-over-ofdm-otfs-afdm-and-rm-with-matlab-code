# ISAC Waveform Contest: Who Will Rule Over OFDM, OTFS, AFDM, and RM? [With MATLAB Code]

ISAC Waveform Contest: OFDM, OTFS, AFDM vs. RM - Who Will Triumph?

Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) is seen as a key enabling technology for the next-generation wireless communication networks. Waveform design directly determines the trade-off between communication reliability and delay-Doppler estimation accuracy. This paper, based on a unified transmission framework, starts from mathematical principles to systematically compare four representative waveforms - Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), Orthogonal Time-Frequency Spatial (OTFS), Affine Fourier Transform (AFDM), and Random Permutation-DFT (RM). By establishing a unified discrete delay-Doppler channel model and linear transceiver operator, it analyzes the theoretical performance differences of each waveform in terms of communication bit error rate (BER), target distance estimation normalized mean squared error (NMSE), and velocity estimation NMSE. Simulation results show that under high Doppler environments, OTFS exhibits significant robustness compared to OFDM, while RM, being the simplest random coding scheme, also possesses certain potential.

The sixth-generation mobile communication system envisions "intelligent connectivity" and "communication with perception." Integrated sensing and communication enable base stations not only to perform data exchange but also to use echo signals to locate and estimate speed around the environment. This trend has spurred research on numerous new waveform designs. Although traditional OFDM has high spectral efficiency and simple receivers, its subcarrier orthogonality is very sensitive to Doppler frequency shifts. OTFS maps information symbols to the delay-Doppler domain, theoretically better able to resist high dynamic channels. AFDM achieves full diversity and robust delay-Doppler estimation using dual chirp parameters in discrete affine Fourier transform (DAFT). RM adopts the lowest additional overhead structure after random permutation followed by DFT, providing another option for hardware-constrained scenarios.
However, most existing comparisons utilize different system models or simulation platforms, making it difficult to fairly evaluate the fundamental differences in waveforms. This paper contributes by establishing a unified linear transceiver framework that allows four waveforms to be compared under identical physical channel conditions, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), and Monte Carlo statistical conditions; defining a unified equivalent channel matrix and perception signal processing method (cross-correlation function, CAF); and providing quantitative results for communication BER and perceived delay/Doppler NMSE from two dimensions. 5.2 Discussion of results communication BER (Figure 1)
At low SNR (&lt;5 dB), the BER of four waveforms is not significantly different, all are dominated by noise.
When the SNR rises to 10 dB, the BER of OFDM slows down significantly. This is due to Doppler shift destroying the orthogonality of subcarriers and residual interference approaching the error floor.
OTFS and AFDM maintain a fast decline in the high SNR region, where AFDM slightly outperforms OTFS, verifying the interference suppression capability of the dual chirp design.
RM performance lies between OFDM and OTFS: The random permutation provides some diversity gain, but lacks explicit matching for time-delayed Doppler.
Distance and Speed NMSE (Figures 2, 3)
At all SNR points, the NMSE of AFDM is lower than that of OFDM by about 6-10 dB (under the same SNR), and the speed NMSE advantage is more significant.
OFDM is limited by the Doppler estimation, and when SNR > 15 dB, the velocity NMSE will reach saturation.
The RM distance and NMSE are similar to those of OTFS in medium-low SNR, but slightly inferior to AFDM at high SNR. This is due to the fact that RM cannot exploit chirp parameters for specific Doppler matching.
Comprehensive interpretation: AFDM and OTFS can significantly enhance perception performance by mapping symbols from the time-frequency domain to a more "matched" domain (time-delay-Doppler or chirp domain), thereby improving their suitability for low complexity, lightweight ISAC devices. Although RM is simple, it provides viable options for low-complexity, light-weight ISAC devices. OFDM remains the most compromised waveform in terms of perception-communication performance, being only suitable for low-speed scenarios.
6. Conclusion The ISAC performance of OFDM, OTFS, AFDM and RM are compared under the unified linear framework in this paper. Both theoretical derivation and Monte Carlo simulation show that:
1  
AFDM and OTFS have the best communication BER and time-delay-Doppler NMSE performance in dual-color dispersion channels, especially suitable for high mobility scenarios.
2  
RM, as a very low complexity random transformation, provides a good compromise between perception accuracy and communication reliability. It is worth further researching its adaptive permutation design.
3  
OFDM performs the worst when Doppler is significant, but if the system already has mature OFDM hardware, partial compensation can still be achieved by increasing pilots or interference suppression algorithms.
Future work can consider more practical pulse shaping, multi-antenna extension, and outdoor real channel modeling. All the simulation models and parameters used in this paper are provided in public code, which facilitates peers to reproduce and improve upon them.
